\section{Introduction}\label{sec1}

Federated learning (FL) promises collaborative model training without centralizing raw data, yet production deployments still struggle with privacy, scalability, and robustness. Classical cross-device FL relies on hub-and-spoke coordinators that ingest plaintext updates, producing single points of failure and exposing contributors to curious servers. Emerging decentralized designs remove the central coordinator but inherit new challenges: clusters must be balanced to mitigate non-IID drift, secure aggregation must tolerate dropouts, and higher-level coordination must propagate consensus models without overwhelming bandwidth or stalling local optimization. Real deployments also demand auditable storage, fault isolation across administrative domains, and observability hooks that explain aggregation delays.

This work presents \emph{SHDFL}, a Secure Hierarchical Decentralized Federated Learning stack that combines the Bonawitz \emph{et al.} secure aggregation protocol with a configurable hierarchy spanning clusters, states, and nations. Each cluster executes the Secure Aggregation Procedure (SAP) so only quorum-protected averages leave the cluster, while bridge nodes gossip External Cluster Models (ECMs) that feed the timer-driven hierarchy. The hierarchy uses asynchronous pull-based dissemination, letting nodes continue local training while periodically fetching scope checkpoints anchored on IPFS and blockchain. Fan-out redundancy, deterministic rosters, and restart-aware scheduling make the system resilient to transient dropouts, and Round Amplification ensures privacy is preserved even when rounds abort.

To support realistic non-IID data, the topology generator partitions datasets via Dirichlet sampling, computes per-node label histograms, and constructs D-cliques with iterative swapping to balance class distributions. The same control plane integrates Hyperledger anchoring, IPFS storage, and Prometheus/Grafana observability, enabling us to trace every round from local SGD through multi-level aggregation. Beyond functionality, we profile the operational envelope through transient dropout campaigns, no-SAP ablations, and privacy analyses that validate quorum enforcement.

Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SHDFL architecture.} We design and implement a secure hierarchical decentralized FL system that unifies cluster-level SAP, bridge-mediated ECM gossip, and multi-scope aggregation suitable for both centralized and decentralized settings.
    \item \textbf{Balanced cluster construction.} We propose a topology pipeline that partitions datasets via Dirichlet sampling and employs label-aware D-clique balancing to reduce drift and communication overhead.
    \item \textbf{Scalable hierarchy.} We present a timer-driven hierarchy with configurable policies (replace/interpolate), fan-out quotas, and asynchronous dissemination so that real deployments can scale across administrative regions without blocking local optimization.
    \item \textbf{Secure aggregation integration.} We adapt the Bonawitz SAP to cluster rounds with quorum enforcement, restart semantics, and Round Amplification analysis, demonstrating enhanced privacy for SHDFL participants.
    \item \textbf{Empirical evaluation.} We report experiments, privacy analyses, and ablation studies—including dropout scenarios and no-SAP runs—that quantify the trade-offs between accuracy, bandwidth, and privacy in our system.
\end{itemize}
